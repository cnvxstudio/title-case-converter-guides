# APA, Chicago, AP, and MLA Title Case: Which Should You Choose?

Choose the style required by your teacher, editor, or publisher. If a project has its own style guide, follow it. Different capitalization does not necessarily mean one result is wrong: standards can make different choices for the same word.

This guide explains the options in [Title Case Converter](https://titlecaseconverter.app/), operated by CNVX. It is not a substitute for a style manual. Examples were checked against the product on September 23, 2026.

## APA title case converter: titles and headings, not entire references

Choose **Title case → APA 7th edition** when APA title capitalization is required. APA title case capitalizes major words, words of four or more letters, and the first word of a title or subtitle. Short verbs such as “is” are not automatically lowercase just because they are short.

Example:

```text
a guide to writing with clarity
→ A Guide to Writing With Clarity
```

APA also uses sentence case in many reference-list titles. Converting the capitalization of a title does not construct or validate a reference: author, date, punctuation, italicization, and source type are separate concerns.

Primary reference: [APA Style — Title Case Capitalization](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/capitalization/title-case).

## Chicago title case converter: the edition matters

Choose **Title case → Chicago 18th edition** for Chicago headline-style capitalization. The first and last words are capitalized. Short articles, some conjunctions, and short prepositions within a title are usually lowercase. In the 18th edition, prepositions of five or more letters are capitalized.

```text
a walk with friends through town
→ A Walk with Friends Through Town
```

Do not assume this option produces an older Chicago edition's result. In this example, “with” remains lowercase and “through” is capitalized. Review compounds and words used as adverbs rather than prepositions.

Primary reference: [Chicago Manual of Style Q&A — longer prepositions and the 18th edition](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/CapitalizationTitles/faq0035.html).

## AP title case converter: composition titles

Choose **Title case → AP Composition titles** for an AP-style book or film title. This option is not a universal setting for every newspaper headline; publications may use their own headline conventions.

AP capitalizes the first and last words, principal words, and prepositions of four or more letters.

```text
a walk with friends through town
→ A Walk With Friends Through Town
```

In this example, AP capitalizes “With,” unlike Chicago 18. Short, context-sensitive words still need review. Follow your publication's requirements rather than assuming a length rule resolves every title.

References: [AP Stylebook](https://www.apstylebook.com/) and [Chicago's official comparison of AP and Chicago preposition rules](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/CapitalizationTitles/faq0035.html). Some detailed manual material requires a subscription.

## MLA title case converter: English-language titles

Choose **Title case → MLA 9th edition** for an English-language title when MLA capitalization is requested. Capitalize first, last, and principal words. Articles, coordinating conjunctions, and prepositions within a title generally remain lowercase.

```text
a walk with friends through town
→ A Walk with Friends through Town
```

MLA keeps “through” lowercase in this context despite its length. The product also handles the phrase “according to” in examples such as:

```text
ideas according to plan
→ Ideas according to Plan
```

Dictionary spelling can matter for compounds. A message or the first line of an untitled poem used as its title may need the source capitalization preserved rather than converted.

Primary reference: [MLA Style Center — Capitalizing a Title](https://style.mla.org/capitalizing-handout-title/).

## Why might I need to edit the result?

Capitalization can depend on meaning, not just spelling. Compare “on” in “Turn On the Light” with “on” in “Notes on Writing.” A formatting tool cannot infer every grammatical role or unfamiliar proper name.

Use this workflow:

1. Choose the required style and convert.
2. Check highlighted warnings, names, abbreviations, and compounds.
3. Add a preferred spelling for a recurring name, or edit the converted output.
4. Copy only after the wording and capitalization match your intended meaning.
